1Answers at a glance

  1. What does a UGC-style video cost per video, across platforms?

    Self-serve prices run wider than they look. JoinBrands can be $25 to $30 a video if you commit to volume, before its platform fee and before your own time; add both and it lands closer to $54 to $86. Collabstr and HFC also have cheap floors, around $50 to $55 and $64, alongside higher typical rates ($205 and $94). Insense runs $187 to $255 with no comparably cheap floor. Benable's $60 to $90 per posted creator has the fee, sourcing, chasing and review built in already, on every video, not just the cheapest one.

    See pricing across platforms →
  2. How do we compare apples to apples when some platforms gift product and charge a fee, others don't?

    The gift is a wash. Every platform in this study ships or reimburses product for a physical good, so it cancels out on both sides. What differs is everything stacked on top: creator fee, platform fee, subscription, rights, and the brand's own hours.

    See pricing across platforms →
  3. What do Home From College and Kale charge, and can we offer in-person retail visits?

    HFC runs $64 to $94 per posted video before product, self-serve below its $15K Premium tier. Kale's brand pricing isn't public and it pays customers, not creators, so it isn't a like-for-like comparator. Yes, a retail-visit variant of LOCAL would work, and cleaner than HFC's version.

    See platform by platform →
  4. A brand at $2,000 a month is comparing us to JoinBrands. Where are we better, and what does JoinBrands cost?

    Self-serve, JoinBrands runs cheaper per video than it first looks, once usage rights are counted in. At volume it's about $54 all-in per post once the brand's own screening and chasing time is priced, versus Benable's roughly $50, and JoinBrands bundles perpetual usage rights at no extra cost, which we currently charge $50 to $100 for. Managed-to-managed is the clearer win: JoinBrands' team-run option is $6,875 at 100 creators a month, ours is $4,999, about 27% cheaper.

    See platform by platform →
  5. Fully loaded, what does JoinBrands actually charge per posted video, and who sets the price?

    The brand sets it. $50 minimum per video pay-as-you-go, or $25 per video committed to a 100-post package. A quoted "$10 a video" blended rate comes from stacking cheap follow-on pieces onto an already-hired creator, not a real per-video price. Usage rights and a year-long Spark code are bundled in at every price point, including $25.

  1. Are content-only files a fair comparison to Benable?

    They're cheaper, and not the same product. soona UGC $89 to $259, Billo $99 per file, full rights, nothing posted. Fair buys if the ask is ad creative, not reach.

Platforms in detail

JoinBrandsSelf-serve marketplaceGifted + feeSelf-serveRights included

A marketplace: the brand posts a campaign like a job listing, creators apply, the brand hires with a click and is charged when a creator accepts. 500K+ creators have signed up and been vetted, though most have completed zero jobs; a real search turned up barely 10 Level 3 creators and about 10 Level 2, everyone else Level 1. Confirmed hands-on in a full demo on Sep 10.

Two ways to pay

  • Content + Posting, pay-as-you-go: $50 minimum per video, no cap
  • Social Blasts, prepaid: commit to 100 posts, $25 each
  • 15% platform fee on top if not on a paid plan

Product and no-shows

  • 95% of brands use reimbursement: creator buys, uploads a receipt, is paid after approval
  • Money sits in JoinBrands' escrow until then
  • Cancel a late or silent creator and the brand gets both the video fee and the reimbursement back

Usage rights, bundled free

  • Perpetual usage rights on every approved video, at any price, including $25
  • TikTok Spark code collected at upload, valid one year, renewable
  • No extra fee at any tier

The "$10 a video" number some brands hear is a blend: once a creator has done a $25 video, the brand can offer them $2 to $5 for a follow-on piece. Average five of those with the original $25 and the blended rate looks like $10 to $13. The real minimum is $50 pay-as-you-go, or $25 committed to a 100-post package.

Managed service costs $4,000 a month on top of creator costs; JoinBrands' own team launches the campaign, briefs, hires, chases and reviews, and the brand only approves the final content. Adoption is thin, under 20 of their 5,000 brands.

Real example: Goli Supplements

1,300 creators done, 1,500 more pending, sent into Target to buy the product and film it. $41.50 per job all in. 1.8 million impressions, roughly 1,000 to 1,400 views a post. Estimated spend: $120,000+ a month.

The real on-time rate

JoinBrands' rep claimed under 5% of jobs run late. On a live campaign pulled up on the call, 51 of 140 jobs on one page were late, and most of the next page too, closer to 30 to 40%.

Where Benable is better

  • Managed by default; JoinBrands' self-serve product leaves finding, filtering, briefing, hiring, chasing and reviewing entirely to the brand, and its managed tier is $4,000 a month on top
  • Creator and content quality: a curated pool versus a marketplace where most creators have completed zero jobs
  • Managed-to-managed price: $4,999 versus JoinBrands' $6,875 at 100 creators a month, about 27% cheaper
  • Working toward a real on-time guarantee against a real 30 to 40% late rate on the other side

Where JoinBrands is better, honestly

  • Usage rights fully bundled at cost, at every price point, including $25; Benable currently charges $50 to $100 extra
  • Scale: 500,000 creators and 5,000 active brands against Benable's tens of thousands and low tens
  • Self-serve per-post price is close once labor is priced in, about $54 all-in versus Benable's $50, and that's before crediting their bundled rights, not a clean win yet
  • No-show protection is structurally cleaner: the creator fronts the money, so a ghost costs the brand nothing but time, not product
Worth stealing

The reimbursement model itself: it puts the financial risk of a no-show on the creator, not the brand. Also worth copying: collecting usage rights preference at the point a creator already wants their payout, rather than negotiating it upfront.

3Pricing across platforms

Product cost is excluded from every number below, on every platform, because every platform ships or reimburses product for a physical good. What differs is everything stacked on top of it: creator fee, platform fee, subscription, rights, and the brand's own hours.

Method: how the numbers below are built

The fully loaded formula

A. Subscription

Divided by videos that month. $0 on pay-as-you-go; the largest line at low volume.

B. Creator payout

The job price the brand sets, or the price the creator lists. $0 on gifted-only collabs.

C. Platform fee

On top of B, on most marketplaces, 8% to 20%.

D. Rights add-ons

Paid usage, whitelisting, Spark codes, raw footage.

E. The brand's hours

Sourcing, briefing, chasing, reviewing. $0 only on managed services.

Fully loaded per video

A + B + C + D + E, product excluded.

Three buckets, one rule each

Gifted only

Benable, Stack Influence, Statusphere, gifted Social Cat, seeding in Aspire and GRIN. Brand pays a platform price and ships product; creator gets no cash.

Gifted plus fee

JoinBrands, Insense, Collabstr, minisocial, paid Social Cat, Home From College. Brand ships product and pays the creator.

Cash for content

Billo, Trend/soona. Fixed price per video, product still shipped, nothing posted to the creator's audience.

Rules worth remembering

  • Quote competitors excluding product, and say so. Adding the gift to Benable's side would double count it.
  • Platforms that reimburse the creator for buying the product fold the product cost into the creator payment. Content-only shops sell a file, not a post, so their number buys less reach and more rights.
  • A posted video lives on the creator's own account and carries their audience. A content-only video is a file the brand posts or runs as an ad. The two are never mixed in this study.
  • Hours per video are assumed: 1.5 hrs on a self-serve marketplace, 3 hrs on a SaaS tool with no marketplace, 0.25 hrs on a managed service. Assumed

  • No cash to creators, no fee stack; the price is the price

  • The post-rate risk is carried, not eaten by the brand

  • The management fee: Stack Influence at $30 to $39 per completed post, about half of Benable's
  • Software alone: GRIN and Social Cat both undercut on subscription price

The sales line, three versions

Against a self-serve marketplace

"Most self-serve platforms run $110 to $250 per posted video plus a fee, plus your own time running every job. The cheapest ones, like JoinBrands at $25 to $30, only get there if you commit to volume upfront, and once you add their fee and the time you spend reviewing and chasing creators yourself, it's closer to $54 to $86. On Benable you ship the product and pay $60 to $90 per creator, and we run it, no volume commitment, no chasing."

Against a content shop

"They sell you a file for about $90 to $100, nobody posts it. We sell a creator posting to their own audience for $60 to $90. If you only need ad creative, buy the file; if you want people talking about you monthly, that's us."

Against a managed service

"Managed programs run $100 to $350 per post with a $3,000 to $10,000 entry, or $30 a post if you take whoever a machine picks in lots of a hundred. We're $60 to $90 per hand-picked creator, ten at a time, month to month."
